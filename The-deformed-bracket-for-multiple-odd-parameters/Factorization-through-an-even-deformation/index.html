Proposition 5.4 (the factorization). The T^{(s)} commute with each other, and

U=\Theta_*T^{(1)}\cdots T^{(r)}.

The product T^{(1)}\cdots T^{(r)} intertwines [\cdot,\cdot]_\beta with [\cdot,\cdot]_{J-\Omega}, and \Theta_* intertwines [\cdot,\cdot]_{J-\Omega} with [\cdot,\cdot]_0. If \Omega\neq0, then \Theta_* is not an automorphism of [\cdot,\cdot]_0.

Text after Proposition 5.4. In particular, if \Omega\neq0, then U(T^{(1)}\cdots T^{(r)})^{-1}=\Theta_* is not of the form \exp(\operatorname{ad} Y) for any even Y\in\mathfrak g_R with \operatorname{ad} Y nilpotent: such a map is an automorphism of [\cdot,\cdot]_0. For r\leq3, \Theta=\mathbb I+\tfrac12\Omega J, so the map \Theta_*-\mathrm{id} has \kappa-degree 2 and its square vanishes. In that case, if \Omega\neq0, the map \Theta_*-\mathrm{id} is not a derivation of [\cdot,\cdot]_0, since otherwise \Theta_*=\exp(\Theta_*-\mathrm{id}) would be an automorphism.

Partially formalized: if Ω ≠ 0, Θ_* is not exp(ad Y) for any even Y with ad Y nilpotent, since exp(ad Y) is an automorphism of [·,·]_0; for r ≤ 3, Θ_* − id is not a derivation of [·,·]_0 when Ω ≠ 0, and in general it is not one when its square vanishes; that for r ≤ 3 the map Θ_* − id has κ-degree 2 and its square vanishes is not formalized. Remark 5.7 (The Fock module). In this remark k,l run over \{1,\ldots,n\}. Let \mathcal F=R_r[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathrm{Cl}(N), with the x_k even and \mathrm{Cl}(N) acting on itself by left multiplication. Sending B_{2k-1}\mapsto\partial_{x_k} and B_{2k}\mapsto x_k represents A_0 on \mathcal F, and composing with \Phi_r represents A_\beta, faithfully, as in the case N=1, r=1 of [Aoi26]. For N=n=r=1 this is the Fock space of [BS17, Section 4], where the mixed relation is realized by an odd derivation and the annihilated element is 1 (see [Aoi26]). Put \eta_{kl}=\Theta_{(2k-1)(2l-1)} and \zeta_{kl}=\Theta_{(2k-1)(2l)}. Since \Theta is \mathbb I plus a matrix with entries of \kappa-degree at least 2, so is \eta, and \eta is invertible. Put \Xi=\eta^{-1}\zeta and \tilde c_k=\sum_l(\eta^{-1})_{kl}c_{2l-1}, and let \Xi^+ and \Xi^- be the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of \Xi. The elements annihilated by every b_{2k-1} are exactly the \varpi\,\sigma with \sigma\in R_r\otimes\mathrm{Cl}(N), where

\varpi=\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac12\sum_{k,l}\Xi^+_{kl}x_kx_l-\sum_kx_k\tilde c_k\Bigr),

a finite sum. Indeed, \mathfrak b_k=\sum_l(\eta^{-1})_{kl}b_{2l-1} acts as \partial_{x_k}+\sum_l\Xi_{kl}x_l+\tilde c_k, and the \mathfrak b_k commute, since J_{(2k-1)(2l-1)}=0. This gives [\tilde c_k,\tilde c_l]=2\Xi^-_{kl}, a central scalar. With \xi=\sum_kx_k\tilde c_k, one has \partial_{x_k}e^{-\xi}=-(\tilde c_k-\tfrac12[\xi,\tilde c_k])e^{-\xi}. The Gaussian factor \exp(-\tfrac12\sum_{k,l}\Xi^+_{kl}x_kx_l) is central in \mathcal F, and \partial_{x_k} multiplies it by -(\Xi^+x)_k, which absorbs the symmetric part of \Xi. The \tilde c_k act by left multiplication. The antisymmetric part \Xi^- cancels the term \tfrac12[\xi,\tilde c_k]=-(\Xi^-x)_k, which arises because the \tilde c_k do not commute. So \mathfrak b_k\varpi=0. Conversely, \varpi is invertible, and \mathfrak b_k(\varpi\sigma')=\varpi\,\partial_{x_k}\sigma' for every \sigma'\in\mathcal F. With \Omega^{\mathrm{oo}}_{kl}=\Omega_{(2k-1)(2l-1)}, \Omega^{\mathrm{oe}}_{kl}=\Omega_{(2k-1)(2l)} and c_{\mathrm o}=(c_{2k-1})_k, the table below lists the cases worked out. In it, \theta, f and \lambda are even central elements of R_r.

\begin{array}{llll} \text{case} & \Theta & \Xi & \varpi \\ \hline r=1 & \mathbb I & 0 & 1-\sum_kx_kc_{2k-1} \\ r=2 & \mathbb I+\tfrac12\Omega J & \tfrac12\Omega^{\mathrm{oo}} & \exp(-\sum_kx_kc_{2k-1}) \\ r=3 & \mathbb I+\tfrac12\Omega J & \tfrac12\Omega^{\mathrm{oo}} & \exp(-\sum_kx_k\tilde c_k) \text{,} \tilde c=(\mathbb I+\tfrac12\Omega^{\mathrm{oe}})c_{\mathrm o} \\ n=1 & \theta\,\mathbb I & 0 & \exp(-\theta^{-1}x_1c_1) \\ N=1 & \mathbb I+f\,\Omega J & \lambda\,(\gamma_{2k-1,0}\gamma_{2l-1,0})_{kl} & \exp(-\sum_kx_k\tilde c_k) \end{array}

For r=2, \eta^{-1}=\mathbb I+\tfrac12\Omega^{\mathrm{oe}} and \Omega^{\mathrm{oe}}c_{\mathrm o} has \kappa-degree 3, so \tilde c=c_{\mathrm o}. For n=1, \Omega=\omega J and \Theta=(1-\omega)^{1/2}\mathbb I. For N=1, (\Omega J)^2 is an even multiple of \Omega J, which gives the form of \Theta. The Gaussian factor \exp(-\tfrac12\sum_{k,l}\Xi^+_{kl}x_kx_l) is 1 unless N,n\geq2 and r\geq4. Indeed, \Xi^+=0 in three cases. For r\leq3, \Theta=\mathbb I+\tfrac12\Omega J and \Xi=\tfrac12(\Omega_{(2k-1)(2l-1)})_{kl}, which is antisymmetric. For n=1, \Theta is a multiple of \mathbb I, so \zeta=0 and \Xi=0. For N=1, \Omega_{uv}=-\gamma_{u,0}\gamma_{v,0} has rank 1, and \Xi_{kl}=\lambda\,\gamma_{2k-1,0}\gamma_{2l-1,0} with a single even \lambda for all k,l, so \Xi is antisymmetric. For N,n\geq2 and r\geq4, \Xi^+ need not vanish. Its part of \kappa-degree 4 is \tfrac18(\Omega^{\mathrm{oe}}\Omega^{\mathrm{oo}}-\Omega^{\mathrm{oo}}(\Omega^{\mathrm{oe}})^{\mathsf T}). At N=n=2, r=4, with \gamma_{1,1^+}=\kappa_1, \gamma_{3,1^-}=\kappa_2, \gamma_{1,1^-}=\kappa_3, \gamma_{4,1^+}=\kappa_4 and the other \gamma_{u,i}=0, one has \Omega_{13}=-\kappa_1\kappa_2 and \Omega_{14}=-\kappa_3\kappa_4, and this gives \Xi^+_{11}=-\tfrac14\Omega_{13}\Omega_{14}=-\tfrac14\kappa_1\kappa_2\kappa_3\kappa_4.
